# Capacity Escalation Policy

Policy BM-POL-005 · Version 3 · Effective 2026-02-01 · Riverbend General Hospital (fictional)

## 1. When to escalate

A placement request must be escalated when no eligible bed exists for the patient. This includes when every suitable bed is occupied, being cleaned, reserved or out of service; when the ward is closed; when no isolation-capable or monitoring-capable bed is free; and when room-sharing compatibility cannot be determined.

## 2. Requirements are never relaxed

Staff and automated systems must not relax a patient's stated requirements in order to find a bed. An isolation requirement, a monitoring requirement, a private-room requirement or the level of care may be changed only by the responsible clinician.

## 3. Who receives an escalation

Escalations go to the bed management coordinator on duty. Outside 07:00 to 19:00 they go to the site nurse manager. The coordinator acknowledges an escalation within 15 minutes and aims to resolve it within 60 minutes.

## 4. What an escalation must contain

An escalation records the patient identifier, the level of care and requirements requested, and the reason no bed could be assigned, including how many candidate beds were rejected and why. It must state clearly that no bed has been assigned.

## 5. Closed wards

A ward may be closed to admissions by the director of nursing, for example during an infection outbreak or a staffing shortfall. Beds in a closed ward must not be offered, even when they show as available. Requests for that level of care are escalated while the ward is closed.
